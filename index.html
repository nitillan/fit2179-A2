Section 01
A Century of Dominance

Australia has contested every Summer Olympics since 1896, winning at least one medal at each appearance. Swimming consistently delivers the lion's share — and the story gets better over time. From modest beginnings, the Dolphins grew into a powerhouse that peaked at the Sydney 2000 home Games and continues to deliver elite results.

Key Moments
A Timeline of Glory
1900 · Paris
Frederick Lane wins two gold medals in swimming — Australia's first-ever Olympic swimming medals.
1912 · Stockholm
Fanny Durack wins women's 100m freestyle — Australia's first female Olympic gold medallist.
1956 · Melbourne
Home Games. Australia wins 8 swimming golds, including Dawn Fraser's first of three consecutive 100m freestyle titles.
2000 · Sydney
Ian Thorpe, Grant Hackett, Susie O'Neill lead Australia to 20 swimming medals — the biggest haul at the time, in front of a roaring home crowd.
2004 · Athens
Ian Thorpe defends his 200m and 400m freestyle crowns. Australia wins 13 swimming golds — a record at a non-home Games.
2021 · Tokyo
Emma McKeon wins 7 medals (4 gold, 3 bronze) — the most medals by any swimmer at a single Olympic Games.
2024 · Paris
Kaylee McKeown defends both backstroke crowns. Ariarne Titmus defends 400m freestyle. Australia wins 18 swimming medals.
